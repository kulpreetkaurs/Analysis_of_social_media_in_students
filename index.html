Analysis of Social Platforms in Students

Analyzing the impact of Social Media Usage on Mental Health, Addiction, and Academic Performance

Tools & Technologies Used

About the Project

Student Social Media Analytics Project analyzes the impact of Social Media usage on Students' Mental Health, Addiction levels, Sleep patterns, and Academic Performance. This project focuses on analyzing the growing influence of social media usage on students' overall well-being and academic performance. With the rapid rise of platforms like Instagram, Facebook, and others, students are spending increasing amounts of time online, which may significantly affect their mental health, sleep quality, productivity, and daily routines.
The study leverages data analytics and visualization techniques to uncover patterns and correlations between social media usage and key lifestyle factors such as mental health scores, sleep duration, addiction levels, and academic outcomes. By transforming raw data into meaningful insights through dashboards and visualizations, the project aims to provide a clear understanding of how digital habits shape student life.
Ultimately, this project serves as a data-driven approach to highlight potential risks, encourage responsible usage, and support informed decision-making for students, educators, and researchers.

Objectives

  • Understand usage patterns across platforms:

    Examine how frequently students use social media, which platforms are most popular, and how usage varies across different demographics such as age, academic level, and region.

  • Analyze relationship between usage and addiction:

    Examine how frequently students use social media, which platforms are most popular, and how usage varies across different demographics such as age, academic level, and region.

  • Evaluate impact on mental health:

    Study how social media usage correlates with mental health indicators, identifying whether higher usage leads to increased stress, anxiety, or lower well-being scores.

  • Identify Addiction trends:

    Investigate the relationship between time spent on social media and addiction scores to understand how excessive usage contributes to dependency behaviors.

  • Assess Effects on Sleep Patterns:

    Analyze how screen time, especially late-night usage, affects sleep duration and quality among students.

  • Measure Academic Performance Impact:

    Explore whether increased social media engagement negatively or positively influences students' academic performance and productivity.

  • Generate Data-Driven Insights:

    Use interactive dashboards (KPI, Usage Analysis, and Advanced Insights) to present clear, actionable insights that help users easily interpret trends and patterns.

  • Support Awareness and Better Decision-Making:

    Provide meaningful conclusions that can help students adopt healthier digital habits and assist educators in understanding behavioral trends.

Tools Used

  • Data Collection:
    1. Google Forms:

      Google Forms was used to design and distribute surveys for collecting primary data directly from students. It enabled structured data gathering on factors such as daily social media usage, sleep patterns, mental health scores, and academic performance. The tool ensured ease of access, real-time responses, and efficient data export for further analysis.

    2. Kaggle:

      Kaggle was utilized to obtain additional datasets and explore publicly available data related to social media usage and student behavior. It also served as a platform for understanding data structures, preprocessing techniques, and benchmarking insights with similar projects.

  • Data Profiling and Analysis:

    Visual Studio Code was used as the development environment, while Python was employed for data cleaning, preprocessing, and exploratory data analysis (EDA). Libraries such as Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, and Seaborn were used to handle missing values, analyze patterns, and generate preliminary visualizations.

  • Data Visualization:

    Tableau was used to create interactive dashboards, including KPI dashboards, usage analysis, and advanced insights. It helped transform complex datasets into visually appealing charts and graphs, making it easier to identify trends, correlations, and actionable insights.

  • Web Development:

    These technologies were used to design and develop the project website. HTML structured the content, CSS enhanced the visual styling and layout, and JavaScript added interactivity, allowing users to navigate dashboards and insights seamlessly.

Key Insights

  • Moderate mental health score (~6.19):

    The average mental health score indicates a moderate level of well-being among students. While not critically low, it suggests that a significant portion of students may be experiencing mild stress, anxiety, or emotional strain—potentially influenced by lifestyle factors such as screen time and online engagement.

  • Noticeable addiction level (~6.36):

    The addiction score reflects a relatively high dependency on social media platforms. This suggests that many students exhibit habitual or compulsive usage patterns, which could interfere with daily activities, focus, and overall productivity.

  • High usage (~4.8 hrs/day):

    Students are spending a considerable amount of time on social media each day. This level of usage goes beyond casual interaction and indicates prolonged screen exposure, which may contribute to reduced physical activity, lower attention spans, and increased digital fatigue.

  • Reduced sleep (~6.8 hrs):

    The average sleep duration is slightly below the recommended 7-9 hours for healthy individuals. This suggests that excessive screen time—especially late-night usage—may be negatively affecting sleep quality and recovery.

  • Interconnected Impact of Usage Patterns:

    A clear relationship emerges between high social media usage, increased addiction levels, and declining well-being indicators. Students who spend more time online are more likely to experience reduced sleep and moderate mental health challenges.

  • Conclusion: Behavioral and Health Implications

    The combined insights suggest that increased social media usage is closely linked with higher addiction levels, reduced sleep duration, and moderate mental health concerns. This highlights the importance of promoting balanced digital habits and raising awareness about the long-term impact of excessive screen time. Increased usage impacts mental health and sleep quality.

Key Insights

  • Instagram dominates usage:

    Among all social media platforms, Instagram emerges as the most widely used by students. Its visually engaging content, reels, and constant updates make it highly addictive and time-consuming. This dominance suggests that students are more inclined toward entertainment-driven platforms rather than informational or professional ones.

  • Most users spend 3-6 hours daily:

    A significant proportion of students fall within the 3-6 hours daily usage range, indicating moderate to high engagement levels. This amount of screen time goes beyond casual browsing and reflects habitual usage, which may reduce time available for academic work, physical activity, and real-world social interactions.

  • Academic performance is affected:

    The data indicates a noticeable impact of social media usage on students academic performance. Excessive time spent online can lead to reduced concentration, procrastination, and lower productivity, ultimately affecting grades and learning outcomes.

  • Moderate conflicts observed:

    Students report experiencing moderate levels of conflict due to social media usage, including disagreements with family or peers and distractions during important activities. While not extremely severe, these conflicts highlight the social and behavioral implications of excessive usage.

  • Conclusion:

    Heavy usage impacts academics and social life. These insights suggest that while social media is deeply integrated into students daily lives, excessive usage—especially on platforms like Instagram—can lead to reduced academic focus, increased dependency, and noticeable social conflicts. Maintaining a balanced approach to usage is essential for overall well-being and productivity.

Key Insights

  • Usage strongly correlates with addiction:

    The analysis reveals a clear positive relationship between the number of hours spent on social media and the level of addiction among users. As daily usage increases, dependency behaviors such as compulsive checking, difficulty staying offline, and reduced self-control become more prominent. This indicates that prolonged exposure significantly contributes to addictive patterns.

  • Sleep improves mental health:

    A strong connection is observed between adequate sleep and better mental health scores. Users who maintain consistent and sufficient sleep schedules tend to report lower stress levels, improved mood, and better emotional stability. This suggests that limiting late-night social media usage can positively impact overall well-being.

  • Undergraduates show more variation:

    Undergraduate students display a wider range of behaviors in terms of social media usage, addiction levels, and mental health outcomes. This variability may be due to differences in lifestyle, academic pressure, social environment, and personal habits, making this group more diverse and less predictable compared to others.

  • Global usage trends observed:

    The data highlights consistent patterns of social media usage across different countries and regions. Despite cultural and geographical differences, users exhibit similar behaviors such as high engagement, preference for visual platforms, and increasing screen time, indicating that social media influence is globally widespread.

  • Conclusion:

    The findings demonstrate that behavioral patterns such as excessive usage and poor sleep habits have a direct and measurable impact on mental health outcomes. Promoting balanced usage and healthier routines is essential to reduce addiction risks and improve overall well-being.
